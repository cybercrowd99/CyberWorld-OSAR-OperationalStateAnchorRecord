# CyberWorld-OSAR
## Operational State Anchor Record

CyberWorld-OSAR is a state representation and authority resolution layer designed to answer one fundamental question:

**"What operational state is being represented, and who has the authority to define that state?"**

CyberWorld-OSAR does not own the world state.  
CyberWorld-OSAR does not become the authority.  

CyberWorld-OSAR records the relationship between:

- a state being represented
- the authority claiming that state
- the evidence supporting the state
- the scope where that state applies
- the lifecycle of that state over time

CyberWorld-OSAR creates a structured record of **represented operational states** without allowing those states to bleed into unrelated contexts.

---

# Why CyberWorld-OSAR Exists

Large systems constantly encounter operational conditions:

- financial restrictions
- platform restrictions
- legal decisions
- safety reviews
- compliance holds
- conflict conditions
- operational failures
- governance decisions

The problem is not recognizing that a state exists.

The problem is answering:

1. Who declared it?
2. Under what authority?
3. Based on what evidence?
4. Where does it apply?
5. When does it expire?

Without these answers, systems create dangerous assumptions:

- a restriction in one lane becomes a restriction everywhere
- a decision by one authority becomes universal authority
- a temporary condition becomes permanent identity

CyberWorld-OSAR exists to prevent those failures.

# Core Principle

## State Is Not Identity

An OSAR record represents a **condition**, not a person.

A state:

- does not represent identity
- does not create ownership
- does not create universal authority
- does not merge unrelated contexts
